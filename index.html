How I lead.

A trusted, inclusive and compassionate business leader with a passion for building high-performing teams that are customer obsessed, solving problems with speed and scale.

Orbital values

In practice

Diagnose, coach, hold the standard, work across teams.

How I diagnose a team

Priority is understanding what matters to every individual and gaining trust. Then five things:

  • How performance is spread across the team.
  • Whether the operating model is fair: territories, routing, support, quotas.
  • Talent and morale.
  • A repeatable playbook.
  • Cross-functional collaboration.

How I coach

  • Managers: my 1:1s are dedicated to their challenges and coaching moments. The forecast is where deals and pacing get scrutinized.
  • ICs: role plays, peer call listening, one-to-ones, weekly deal strategy and pulse sessions.

How I handle underperformance

Clear, fair and early on expectations of how and what we need to do. Coaching, written feedback and the PIP process are outlined for transparency.

The standard100% is good. 101% onwards is great.

How I work across teams

One team, one customer journey. Inclusive, accountable, a systems thinker and a thoughtful challenger of GTM models.

  • Involve all teams early in key deals.
  • Agree how we work together.
  • Lock in regular 1:1s and QBRs.
  • Seek regular feedback on collaboration.

7 · AI in the sales motion

Five places agents take work off people and raise output.

I led the build and testing of an agent for research, deal planning and sequenced outreach, then rolled it out in stages across the enterprise team. The rule I use: agents take repeatable, low-risk work, and people take ambiguity, trust, money and relationships.

1 · Research, signals and white space

One agent researches every named account, watches for signals and trigger events, and maps white space. The focus is the accounts nobody is working today, so every account gets a touch and sellers spend their time with buyers.

2 · Give sellers their time back

Agents write pre-meeting briefs, take notes, update the CRM and draft follow-up. The goal is 80% of seller time with customers.

3 · Make the buyer’s path clear

Agents answer product and security questions, draft order forms and keep a shared deal room current. Buyers move on their own time, and a person steps in at set triggers.

4 · Business value agent

Most selling happens without the seller in the room. After every meeting, the agent builds the business case in the customer’s language and strategic objectives, so the champion has something to carry and we sound like the people we sell to.

5 · Inbound lead agents

Agents respond to inbound leads at once, qualify them and route them to the right seller, so no lead waits and sellers pick up only the ones worth a conversation.

Time given back only turns into revenue if there is pipeline to work, which is why the research agent matters as much as time back. I would start with one workflow per agent, measure the time saved and the effect on pipeline and win rate over the first 90 days, then expand, with a person reviewing anything a customer sees until it has earned trust.

2 · Command of the message

Why change, why us, why now.

Why change

Real estate legal work is document-heavy and scattered across title, survey, leases and email. Lawyers are stuck in repetitive review, reports vary by team and office, and fee pressure pushes out-of-scope work onto the firm.

Estate-wide and full-portfolio reviews stay undone because they are too costly by hand, so the cost of staying put is slower closings and work that never gets started.

Why us

Orbital is built for real estate and holds the official ALTA policy forms, endorsements and the 2026 survey standards, so title and survey work is checked against the source wording. It plots legal descriptions and easements on a map, drafts from a firm’s own forms, and coordinates the deal.

Outputs link back to the source documents and provisions, so a lawyer can check the work. Customers choose it for real estate depth over general tools.

Why now

AI in legal has moved from pilots to practice, and real estate is where it is landing first. The firms that move first win more deals with the same team, hold fees while costs fall, and catch title and survey issues earlier. Orbital is committed to winning real estate law, and the prize is making real estate liquid: speed to certainty in diligence helps deals close and capital move.

I would turn this into a one-page narrative for each buyer persona, built around business value and not around a list of features. The proof for each segment needs real customer outcomes, which I would ask Orbital for.

6 · Where we need to overcome potential challenges

Eight things to work through in the new segments.

1 · The economic buyer is different in each segment

A partner, a general counsel and a lender’s credit team buy differently. Selling to the wrong person looks like a long cycle.

2 · The gates

Larger buyers raise security, data residency and AI governance questions: model providers, data retention and training, and a way to switch AI off. Orbital is UK-based, so US buyers may ask where data is held. In SLED these gates set the pace of the deals.

3 · Competition from general legal AI

Harvey, Legora and others will be in the room. If “why us” is only a list of use cases, Orbital competes on breadth. It wins on real estate depth.

4 · Core deals can crowd out new segments

If comp pays the same for an easy core deal and a slower occupier or lender deal, sellers will choose the easy one. At LinkedIn Learning, quota was not set at the rep level in the first year, and it took a 1.25X multiplier to change behavior.

5 · Adoption after the signature

Adoption can lag. If implementation and customer success meet the buyer only after signing, speed to value suffers and renewals get harder.

6 · Deal size

The average deal is about $70K. If occupiers and lenders land below that, they will pull the average down. The $100K+ and $250K+ goals on the Orbital math tab need larger named accounts.

7 · In-house use can cut outside counsel spend

Self-serve at Grosvenor and M&S reduces reliance on outside legal. Winning in-house can shrink the work of Orbital’s own law firm customers. I would agree how Orbital handles that with law firms before I push in-house hard.

8 · US proof is thinner than UK proof

The in-house proof is all UK, for example Grosvenor and M&S. The US proof is law firms, Seyfarth and Vinson & Elkins. Legora’s Cadastral already has US owners such as JLL and AvalonBay. We need US-based owner references before we go head to head.
