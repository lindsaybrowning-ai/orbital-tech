Leadership · My style

How I lead.

A trusted, inclusive and compassionate business leader with a passion for building high-performing teams that are customer obsessed, solving problems with speed and scale.

My principles of work

  • Invest in people
  • Customer obsessed
  • Adaptability and agency
  • Curiosity and efficiency
  • Seek relentless improvement

Orbital values

  • Bold and ambitious
  • Power to our people
  • Question or commit
  • Eat that frog

In practice

Diagnose, coach, hold the standard, work across teams.

How I diagnose a team

Priority is understanding what matters to every individual and gaining trust. Then five things:

  • How performance is spread across the team.
  • Whether the operating model is fair: territories, routing, support, quotas.
  • Talent and morale.
  • A repeatable playbook.
  • Cross-functional collaboration.

How I coach

  • Managers: my 1:1s are dedicated to their challenges and coaching moments. The forecast is where deals and pacing get scrutinized.
  • ICs: role plays, peer call listening, one-to-ones, weekly deal strategy and pulse sessions.

How I handle underperformance

Clear, fair and early on expectations of how and what we need to do. Coaching, written feedback and the PIP process are outlined for transparency.

The standard100% is good. 101% onwards is great.

How I work across teams

One team, one customer journey. Inclusive, accountable, a systems thinker and a thoughtful challenger of GTM models.

  • Involve all teams early in key deals.
  • Agree how we work together.
  • Lock in regular 1:1s and QBRs.
  • Seek regular feedback on collaboration.

8 · SDR outbound in the market

Teams are getting smaller. Orbital’s is large.

Headcount is falling

  • 36% of 560+ venture-backed B2B software companies cut SDR headcount in the year to April 2025. 44% held flat and 19% added. That is the highest cut rate of any sales role (Emergence Capital survey, via SaaStr).
  • On LinkedIn Sales Navigator data, SDR headcount at a panel of target accounts fell 22.7% over eight quarters while AE and full-cycle seats rose 10.8%. 77% of teams with 20 or more SDRs cut, by 21.4% on average (Regie.ai).

What is left works hybrid

  • Typical benchmarks: a median of 8 meetings booked per SDR per month, 20 or more in the top quartile. 40% to 55% of meetings become opportunities. Full productivity takes 90 to 180 days, and 5 to 6 months in enterprise (Boomerang, which cites Bridge Group, RepVue and Gong).
  • SDR to AE ratios run from 1 to 2 up to 1 to 1 depending on the motion, with enterprise closer to 1 to 1.

Where Orbital sits

  • 17 SDRs to 8 AEs is about 2.1 SDRs per AE. The sources above put it at 0.25 to 1.
  • The open question is how big the human team needs to be as agents take on more of the research and first-touch work.

What I would do

  • Hold the SDR team to SQLs reaching discovery at a set share of pipeline, then decide the right size from that.
  • Let agents take research and first-draft outreach so people spend their time on calls with lawyers.
  • Pair SDRs with AEs on named accounts, with marketing events and ABM feeding the same accounts.

Market figures come from vendor and analyst blogs, so treat them as directional. Sources: Emergence Capital via SaaStr, Regie.ai, and Boomerang.

2 · Command of the message

Why change, why us, why now.

Why change

Real estate legal work is document-heavy and scattered across title, survey, leases and email. Deals stall on handoffs, and a team cannot add people as fast as deal volume grows.

The cost of staying put is slower closings and lawyers’ time spent on routine review.

Why us

Orbital is built for real estate. Its website lists asset intelligence that links title, survey and lease data to a property, drafting and negotiation from a firm’s own forms, and deal coordination.

Outputs link to sources with audit trails, and it lists ISO 27001 and SOC 2 Type 2.

Why now

AI in legal is moving from pilots to budgets. General legal AI vendors such as Harvey and Legora are targeting the same buyers, so category positions are being set now.

Orbital has fresh capital ($60M Series B in January 2026) and is expanding in the US.

I would turn this into a one-page narrative for each buyer persona, built around business value and not around a list of features. The proof for each segment needs real customer outcomes, which I would ask Orbital for.

4 · Where it may be challenging

Six things that can slow the new segments.

1 · The economic buyer is different in each segment

A partner, a general counsel and a lender’s credit team buy differently. Selling to the wrong person looks like a long cycle.

2 · The gates

Larger buyers raise security, data residency and AI governance questions: model providers, data retention and training, and a way to switch AI off. Orbital is UK-based, so US buyers may ask where data is held. In SLED these gates set the pace of the deals.

3 · Competition from general legal AI

Harvey, Legora and others will be in the room. If “why us” is only a list of use cases, Orbital competes on breadth. It wins on real estate depth.

4 · Core deals can crowd out new segments

If comp pays the same for an easy core deal and a slower occupier or lender deal, sellers will choose the easy one. At LinkedIn Learning, quota was not set at the rep level in the first year, and it took a 1.25X multiplier to change behavior.

5 · Adoption after the signature

Adoption can lag. If implementation and customer success meet the buyer only after signing, speed to value suffers and renewals get harder.

6 · Deal size

The average deal is about $70K. If occupiers and lenders land below that, they will pull the average down. The $100K+ and $250K+ goals on the Orbital math tab need larger named accounts.

Before the new segments get a pod.
